# Decide whether to leave a senior job to join a startup as employee four

> No option is recommended, the decision is yours · Description partly confirmed · Heuristic · No effectiveness checks found

## Task

You are 34, senior and well paid at a large company, with a mortgage and a wife on parental leave. A founder you like offers 60% of your pay plus 1.5% equity at a startup with about 14 months of money. You swing between yes and no every day.

- **Decision:** stay, join as offered, or join on changed terms.
- **Decider:** you, in practice jointly with your wife (A1).
- **Objectives (proposed by the-method; keep or strike):** household safety (mortgage, buffer); four-year expected pay; learning and growth; enjoying the work; this founder; family time in the baby's first year; options if it fails; your wife's peace of mind.
- **Success condition:** objectives agreed with your wife, terms verified in writing, limits written before deciding, decision made by a set date with recorded reasons.

## Problem Class

- **Class:** personal high-stakes choice among a few alternatives, under uncertainty, with several conflicting objectives.
- **Recognise it by:** a few identifiable options; objectives that pull in different directions; uncertain key outcomes; hard to reverse, with effects on money and family.
- **Not this class when:** one objective decides it and the question is factual (budgeting); the choice is repeated many times (a search problem); the other side's moves are the core (negotiation).
- **Boundary:** swapping "a one-off choice" for "a repeated choice" changes the steps; swapping "startup offer" for "moving abroad" does not.
- **Candidates considered:**
  - multi-objective choice under uncertainty - chosen: meets every criterion above.
  - job-offer negotiation - rejected: terms matter, but the open question is whether to go at all, not how much to ask for.
  - household financial risk planning - rejected: covers one objective only; it feeds S5 and S6.
- **Other classes in this task:** negotiating offer terms (S4)

## Method

- **Name:** PrOACT (Smart Choices, with Even Swaps)
- **Applies when:** a decider or household chooses among identifiable alternatives and can state several objectives.
- **Steps:**
  - **M1.** Define the problem: what is decided, by whom, by when.
  - **M2.** Specify objectives: the ends you care about, not the means.
  - **M3.** Create alternatives beyond yes or no.
  - **M4.** Build a consequences table: alternatives against objectives, with checked facts.
  - **M5.** Make trade-offs: drop dominated alternatives, then use even swaps until one dominates.
  - **M6.** Clarify uncertainty: name key uncertainties and set out scenarios.
  - **M7.** State your risk tolerance and test each alternative's worst case against it.
  - **M8.** Name the later decisions this one opens or closes.
- **Verify the result:** the table is complete, the choice is not dominated, its worst case is within tolerance, and everyone affected confirmed the objectives.

### Choice

- **PrOACT** - chosen: handles several objectives, risk tolerance and linked decisions explicitly; steps confirmed by the authors' book (R1).
- **WRAP (Decisive)** - alternative. Switch when: the table is complete and you still swing daily (A6). Basis: steps named by one reliable secondary source (R9); authors apply it to job decisions (R10).
- **Regret Minimization Framework** - alternative. Switch when: PrOACT leaves alternatives practically tied within your limits, as a tie-breaker. Basis: author's own account (R7); core premise only partly replicated (R8).
- **Depends on:** A6

## Assumptions

- **A1.** Your wife shares the decision in practice. If wrong: Decider and S1, S2 change.
- **A2.** Her leave ends in autumn 2027, so household income stays reduced until then. If wrong: cash-flow figures in S5 change.
- **A3.** The equity is stock options with four-year vesting and a one-year cliff; you have not seen the terms. If wrong: S4 and S5 change.
- **A4.** The 14 months is the founder's own figure, with no committed next round. If wrong: the scenarios in S5 change.
- **A5.** You could get a comparable senior role within about six months if the startup fails. If wrong: limits in S6 tighten.
- **A6.** Your swinging comes from unclear objectives and trade-offs, not from fear that persists after the analysis. If wrong: switch to WRAP.
- **A7.** You have a few weeks before you must answer. If wrong: fit S1-S9 into one week, keeping S4, S6 and S9.

## Plan

- [ ] **S1 (M1, M8).** With your wife, write one page: what is decided, the decision date, who decides, and linked decisions (her return to work, the mortgage, a job search if the startup fails). Done when: you both agree on the page.
- [ ] **S2 (M2).** You and your wife each list objectives separately, starting from the proposed list; then merge and strike. Note which objective your dad's and your friend's views each speak to. Done when: an agreed list of four to seven objectives exists.
- [ ] **S3 (M3).** List at least four alternatives: stay; join as offered; join on changed terms (more cash, more equity, a start after her leave); fix your current job (an internal move or a new scope). Done when: each alternative is written specifically.
- [ ] **S4 (-).** Ask the founder in writing for: option type, vesting, cliff, strike price, fully diluted share count, last valuation, preferences, runway assumptions and next-round plan; propose your changed terms. Why: 1.5% means nothing without these. Done when: you have written answers.
  - Needs its own Method: negotiating job offer terms. Run the-method on this step when you reach it.
- [ ] **S5 (M4, M6).** Fill the table of alternatives against objectives. For money, model three startup scenarios (fails within 14 months, raises and grinds on, a good exit) with your household's monthly cash flow at 60% pay. Done when: every cell is filled, with money in figures.
- [ ] **S6 (M7).** Before choosing, write your limits: minimum monthly income, the savings buffer you will not touch, the longest job search you can absorb. Test each alternative's worst scenario against them. Done when: each alternative is marked inside or outside your limits.
- [ ] **S7 (-).** Spend two or three days working alongside the founder, and live on 60% of your take-home for one month. Write down beforehand what you expect, then compare. Why: a proxy try-and-check for the heuristic mark. Done when: notes on expected against actual exist for both.
- [ ] **S8 (-).** Have a fee-only financial planner check the scenarios and the equity's tax treatment, and an employment lawyer read the offer and your current contract's non-compete and IP clauses. Why: specialist review. Done when: both reviews are done and their findings noted.
- [ ] **S9 (M5).** Drop dominated and out-of-limit alternatives, then use even swaps on the rest. Decide on the S1 date, and write your reasons plus tripwires that reopen the decision. Done when: the decision, its reasons and its tripwires are written down.

## Verification

- **Outcome:** not checkable at decision time.
- **Process check:** objectives agreed with your wife, terms in writing, limits written before choosing, specialist reviews done, decision made on the set date.
- **Review on:** six months after deciding, or at a tripwire (runway under six months with no term sheet, or the buffer touched).
- **If it fails:** still swinging after S9: switch to WRAP (A6); a fact changes: return to S5.
